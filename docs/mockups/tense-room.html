What the learner has to read

The time adverb is gone, so the verb form is the only cue. That is Processing Instruction: interpret first, produce later. Guessing from the noun or the topic gets you nothing.

The Hebrew is withheld during the ask. In the fill-the-gap room the Hebrew tells you; here it would give the answer away for four of the six forms.

Why not the other two shapes

  • ✕Connect lines. A drag across a small screen is expensive to aim, and there is no sane TalkBack story for a line.
  • ✕Sort six cards into three piles. Elimination. Once Yesterday is full you can place the rest without reading a single verb — the exact failure docs/fill-the-gap.md was written to stop.
  • ✓Kept from the sorting idea: the timeline stays on screen, placed sentences pile up under their heading, and the sorted timeline is the end-of-round payoff.

Content gap, flagged not hidden

Both present-simple specimens above are the copula — is. For the contrast a Hebrew speaker actually gets wrong, present simple against present progressive, we need habitual lines with lexical verbs, and 17 of the 23 third-person -s forms are absent from the entire corpus. The room cannot ship its most important column until those are authored — that is task #71, not a detail.

Why strip at all. With the adverb in place the sentence has two cues to the same meaning and English lets you ignore the harder one. Remove the redundant cue and the inflection is load-bearing. It is also the one thing a wrong tap can honestly explain without a grammar lecture: we hid a word, here it is.

Not when — whether it is finished

From Level 5 the three destinations stop being clock times. Hebrew has three tenses, no grammatical aspect and no perfect, so one Hebrew word covers four English forms — the corpus shows חלב serving past simple, past progressive, past perfect and past perfect progressive alike. This is the stage where the Hebrew is shown after the answer, to make the missing distinction visible rather than to hint at it.

Finishedנגמר

We visited the doctor yesterday.

Over and done. Hebrew marks it the same way English does — this is the one destination the learner can reach from Hebrew alone.

The four states of a turn

Numbered because a turn really does run in this order. The wrong-tap treatment is lifted straight from ClozeScreen — struck through, dimmed, disabled, with stateDescription set for TalkBack — so the two rooms behave the same way under the finger and under the screen reader.

Which way the timeline runs

The timeline follows the ambient layout direction, so in the Hebrew UI the past sits on the right. The English sentence inside each card stays LTR regardless — that is EnglishContent, which pins LocalLayoutDirection to Ltr for English text inside a Hebrew screen. Same item, both directions:
